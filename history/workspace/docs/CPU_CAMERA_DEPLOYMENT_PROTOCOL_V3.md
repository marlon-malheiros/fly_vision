# Portable Linux CPU and full raw-camera replay — V3

Declared 2026-10-21 before generating the new camera fixtures. This is a separate engineering configuration, following completion of the frozen numerical generalization/convergence component. All earlier archives, models, criteria and results stay preserved. The user selected portable Linux CPU first; strict CPU equations with the original 1-ms solver are the default. GPU work remains separately optional. No closed-loop control is introduced.

Export source, the original graph/parameters, calibration interface, replay CLI and three complete two-second camera fixtures. Each fixture uses historical training room s01, forward speed 16.5 mm/s and yaw 0, +20 or −20°/s, with original 0.5–1.5-s motion. These are software regression inputs, not new held-out classifier training or evidence of physical-camera calibration. The existing 70-sample episode readout may be enabled only with independently supplied known speed; do not change its coefficients, cutoffs, window or decisions.

Synthetic cameras have 320×320 monochrome uint8 frames, pixel center (159.5,159.5), equidistant projection r=fθ, a circular 95° half-field with radius 158 pixels, optical azimuth −80°/left and +80°/right, zero optical elevation and original 0.3-mm binocular baseline. Camera right is increasing body azimuth and image down is negative elevation. Compute the frozen retinal rays from the declared eye charts, project them into these cameras and require all four interpolation vertices within the lens aperture. Pixels outside the lens are opaque housing; no missing required retinal ray is filled. Save aperture masks and projection provenance. Status is **SYNTHETIC_TEST_ONLY**.

Exposure is 1,000 µs centered at the integer timestamp. Integrate linear radiance with two-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature before encoding raw pixels as round(255√luminance); calibration uses LUT (code/255)² before bilinear interpolation. Compare four-point exposure integration at fixed times 0.5, 1 and 1.5 s as a descriptive quadrature diagnostic. Source projection and exposure are synthetic assumptions, not fitted biological optics. Global exposure-midpoint timestamps start at 1,000,000,000 ns and advance 5,000,000 ns; left/right timestamps are equal. Declared adapter skew/jitter bounds are 100,000/20,000 ns. These tolerances do not establish actual camera synchronization.
