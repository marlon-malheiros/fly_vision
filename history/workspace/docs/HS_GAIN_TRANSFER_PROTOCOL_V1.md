## Evidence and hypothesis

[Drews et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.10.035) report fast spatial contrast compression upstream of T4/T5. This supports testing divisive gain control, but supplies neither a MaleCNS electrical conductance nor the parameter calibrated here. The distinct [Matulis et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7003801/) study reports heterogeneous temporal adaptation; do not claim a shared instantaneous operation reconstructs all input neuron types. Its raw data are not publicly deposited according to its availability statement, so do not invent those recordings.

One candidate normalizes the image-derived rectified ON/OFF contrasts **before** their delay and multiplicative motion computation: u'_p = u_p*sqrt((1+sigma^2)/(sigma^2+E)), where E is the local mean of u_ON^2+u_OFF^2 over the inherited seven-degree pooling footprint. This is a causal, spatial, phenomenological upstream operation, not newly identified neurons/synapses or a fully physiological frontend. The footprint, common ON/OFF treatment and instantaneous gain are explicit uncalibrated assumptions. Delay remains 30 ms, chemical graph and neural dynamics unchanged. Rectified final source drive is clipped to the inherited [0,1] interface; retain clipping counts. Stationary input must remain zero. No true contrast, frequency, yaw, speed or direction label is supplied to frontend inference.

This differs from the prior engineering output-energy division and neural readout normalization: a positive semisaturation is now calibrated from neuronal measurements, and normalization occurs before temporal filtering and correlation. This distinction does not imply prior normalization failures were irrelevant or that a new variant must succeed.
