# Fixed readouts of the published neural motion decoder V1

1 November 2026. Freeze before examining the walking decoder arrays. Canonical responses have already been inspected; walking development simulations are in progress. This is a new development analysis, not a pre-test confirmation. The full neural model and native decoder remain frozen.

Use all eight plus/minus 60-degree/s canonical axis responses (two texture phases) to fit one zero-intercept two-by-two least-squares conversion from the native decoder's spatial mean changes into the canonical stimulus coordinates in degrees/s. Keep the four plus/minus 2 controls out of this conversion and report their transfer errors. This corrects the interface's unspecified training scale; it is an artificial observation/readout mapping, not calibration of neuron conductances or a change to biological parameters.

Three fixed walking readers use the real decoded field in the central extent-10 hexagon. All use a causal 100-ms mean minus the same mean 200 ms earlier, computed separately at every site and eye. No actual speed, scene, turn label, depth, or onset flag enters inference. The score is a change in estimated yaw, so sustained-yaw inference remains a separate limitation.

Reader 1 averages native horizontal change in each eye and computes (R-L)/2. Reader 2 transforms native motion into world azimuth/elevation velocity and removes the forward-translation component using the known eye ray direction: yaw = -azimuth_velocity + sin(azimuth)/(cos(elevation)*sin(elevation)*cos(azimuth)) * elevation_velocity. Average using squared vertical forward-translation basis weights. Reader 3 uses the same site estimates and fixed weights with a weighted median. Exclude geometrically singular zero-weight sites. These are artificial geometric readers of neural activity; they are not anatomically reconstructed HS/bIPS/DNp15 neurons.

Evaluate the inherited 1-degree/s absolute cutoff, the 1.19-second endpoint and the maximum straight/stationary alarm over every callback in [1,3). Also determine the minimum common cutoff compatible with <=10% episode false alarms in each speed/stationary group; report what positive endpoint sensitivity remains. This is descriptive calibration on two inspected rooms. Do not choose a best-performing reader or threshold and call these rooms held-out validation. If any candidate advances, freeze its exact coefficients and cutoff before independent environments and numerical controls.
