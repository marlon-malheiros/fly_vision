**Route C — conditional route to a stronger compensation/degeneracy study.**

Use this only if the requested scientific output is explicitly beyond the
original diagnostic. Our existing timing example already meets a limited
computational compensation definition. Calling it a different neural mechanism
requires additional evidence; merely finding another parameter vector is not
sufficient.

1. **Choose the level of claim first.** A model-only compensation study can use
   simulated stimuli and engineering tolerances. A biological claim first needs
   a functional contract supported by Route B or equivalent independent evidence.
   Preserve both primary and mean discrepancies when defining that contract;
   do not drop a biologically relevant output just because it prevents a positive
   search result. If no adequately validated baseline exists, stop at this gate.
2. **Specify nontrivial mechanisms and held-out predictions.** Quotient unused
   parameters and exact filter-pole permutations. Declare parameter/anatomical
   bounds and final E/I differences before the search, reporting both absolute
   and normalized conductance differences, their time/condition locations and
   physiological interpretation. The current “both ≥10% somewhere” gate is a
   computational definition; different trials can set its two maxima. Decide
   prospectively whether the new scientific claim requires simultaneous changes,
   different connectivity, distinct response dynamics under intervention, or
   another concrete mechanism distinction.
3. **Partition the evidence.** Existing 78 model conditions are development
   inputs already examined. Freeze a fresh stimulus or recorded-cell test set,
   including stationary/contrast/speed cases justified by the source model's
   valid operating range. Define output equivalence, physiological acceptance,
   numerical tolerances and candidate-selection rules before generating new
   candidates. Repeated peeking at that test set converts it to development data.
4. **Permit one bounded search with a new hypothesis.** If nonlinear compensation
   is the hypothesis, a constrained continuation from the fixed baseline is a
   different test from the old straight SVD steps. Predeclare exactly two branches
   (common or opposing log-gain changes), fixed continuation steps, one optimizer
   and initialization, and a hard objective-evaluation budget before execution.
   As a concrete planning ceiling, use 500 evaluations per branch, logging every
   candidate and preserving an interruption checkpoint; finalize this budget
   against a cost-only timing probe, before inspecting new candidate responses.
   Keep the frozen scientific gates and bounds. No new starts, model families or
   relaxed thresholds after seeing failure. This is model-output optimization,
   expressly outside the original no-optimization diagnostic.
5. **Test once on untouched inputs.** Select at most one candidate per branch by
   the predeclared development rule. Verify independent integration, input
   geometry, threshold configuration and all response/mechanism gates. Evaluate
   the held-out set once. Failed candidates remain failures; no retuning on the
   held-out scores. Numerical equivalence of weak baseline responses alone is
   not evidence of physiological adequacy.
6. **Make a discriminating prediction.** Before evaluating it, choose one feasible
   perturbation justified by the mechanism difference—for example a defined
   reduction of a modeled E or I conductance. Demonstrate that qualified models
   predict measurably different outcomes under the same intervention while
   agreeing on the functional contract. A simulated intervention gives a
   testable hypothesis. Identifying which mechanism the animal uses requires
   suitable physiological or intervention data.
7. **Close either way.** Report confirmed computational alternatives and their
   limits, or “no qualifying alternative within the declared search/budget.”
   Do not conclude uniqueness/impossibility from failure. If the search fails,
   retain the validated timing-compensation note and Route A/B outputs. Do not
   rebuild the renderer as the default response to a negative parameter search.

require conductance, synaptic or perturbation measurements and independently
supported bounds. Public voltage traces alone generally do not identify both
internal conductances. Data availability and experimental feasibility must be
resolved before promising this stronger endpoint.
